# Lightweight agent and market simulation research — 2026-10-08

**Status:** Research and proposed implementation guidance only. No algorithms were imported, no Bunting implementation was changed, and no benchmark or historical calibration was executed for this review.  
**Bunting source baseline:** [main@411e0baf](https://github.com/andrewkoumoudjian/bunting/tree/411e0baf767d4c81c27308f25117f1013bbd4161).  
**Existing authority:** [engine roadmap](../plans/2026-10-07-evidence-led-core-roadmap.md), [independent core audit](2026-10-07-independent-core-architecture-audit.md), [ADR 0019](../adr/0019-bunting-engine-package-owns-orderbook-rs.md), [ADR 0024](../adr/0024-discrete-matching-interval-fairness.md), [proposed ADR 0028](../adr/0028-proposed-headless-run-authority.md).

## Recommendation

Keep Bunting's **one Rust authoritative market engine** and its private OrderBook-rs matcher integration. Do not add another production matching engine, shadow economic ledger, or C++ sidecar. Implement causal, deterministic, lightweight synthetic liquidity and heterogeneous agents *outside the venue authority*, submitting ordinary authorized participant commands. Research queue-reactive state-dependent point processes first; retain simple seeded zero-intelligence flow as the falsifiable baseline, then introduce inventory-aware market makers, execution agents, cross-venue arbitrage and, only if necessary, Hawkes excitation.

The implementation priority is **correct live mutable matching/atomic accounting and replay, then causal scheduling and empirically credible agent flow**, not a premature thread-per-agent or lock-free rewrite. External throughput numbers below are authors' own claims for different workloads; none measures Bunting's end-to-end command + economic commit path.

### Performance claims and interpretation

- TALON documents 125 simulated agents (100 zero-intelligence, 20 market makers, five momentum traders), with a global discrete-event scheduler. Its published performance/mismatch observations are **upstream self-reports** and do not establish Bunting performance.
- The C++ exchange-simulator README claims approximately **20.3 million orders/second** and sub-100-nanosecond median matching on Apple M4. Those results exclude Bunting's risk, durable commit, recovery, execution reports and full agent lifecycle; compare only after identical semantic workload and hardware are imposed.
- There is no defensible shared performance ranking for these projects: pure book operations, whole simulation events and market replay are different units. Profile CPU cycles/event, allocations, memory/event, complete event/commit throughput, p50/p99 latency and replay hash rather than copying third-party numbers into Bunting's SLOs.

## Smallest useful agent engine

**Causal process.** All participant decisions derive only from committed, *delivered* market/public events, actor-specific private events, scheduled news and internal agent state. Decisions produce bounded intents through the existing QUARCC adapter and ordinary Bunting authentication/risk/commit path. An order-flow generator is itself an explicitly funded/authorized participant or bounded set of participants, never privileged mutation of a hidden book.

**Scheduler.** Use a logical-time min-heap keyed by (time, phase/priority, stable sequence, participant/event ID). Events: news/calendar changes, exchange processing, public feed delivery, private execution report delivery, scheduled policy wake, agent-generated command admission, and timeouts. Record all exogenous inputs and seeds. Priority at equal timestamps must match the published competition admission policy, not accidentally change ADR 0024. Avoid wall-time sleeps and thread-per-agent execution inside the transition core. O(log N) enqueue/dequeue replaces current O(N) next-wake scans; benchmark actual workloads before selecting a custom priority queue.

**Queue-reactive null-to-realism ladder.** Start with a statistically falsifiable Poisson/zero-intelligence null. Estimate a bounded state bucket s from delivered or authoritative book state as appropriate to *generator role*: spread, bid/ask queues, imbalance, session/regime and listing. Estimate rates for order arrival, cancellation and marketable trade types. Let Λ(s)=Σ_e λ_e(s), sample Δt ~ Exp(Λ), then sample event type with probability λ_e(s)/Λ(s). Choose side, price offset and size from conditioned distributions. When state changes, resample according to the specified continuous-time model and deterministic RNG stream. Treat zero intensity, extreme hazards, halts, empty books and volume conservation explicitly. Each generated cancel must target a genuinely owned resting order.

**Market-maker layer.** Model outstanding quote IDs, queue position, cancel/replace, inventory limits, markout and adverse selection. Implement Avellaneda–Stoikov and GLFT in dedicated policies with unit-consistent volatility/time/risk-aversion/arrival parameters and explicit tick rounding; do not relabel a spread heuristic as optimal control. Keep a simpler inventory-skew policy as a baseline.

**Heterogeneous participants.** Add informed/value agents with explicitly delayed noisy observations, institutional TWAP/VWAP/POV or parent-order execution under true book constraints, momentum/mean reversion, and arbitrage that compares executable quotes across *distinct ListingKeys* after fees, latency, inventory and settlement constraints. A participant's displayed consolidated NBBO must be assembled from delivered, timestamped venue observations, not from future authoritative books. Same economic InstrumentId may list at multiple venues without merging their FIFO queues.

**Optional Hawkes layer.** Only introduce a state-dependent cross-exciting intensity after showing simpler state-dependent Poisson misses clustering/inter-arrival diagnostics. Implement a true conditional-intensity event-time sampler with bounded history/decay and restoreable RNG, rather than incrementing an intensity counter on fixed wakes.

**Public information boundary.** A causally reconstructed public projection per subscribed listing is permitted as an *agent observation cache*, never an additional venue book or matching authority. De-duplicate its event sequence and show that late feed delivery cannot leak later state. Maintain participant-private fills/position reports separately. Exchange latency, feed latency and order-admission delay have distinct distributions/recorded event times.

## Integration slices and acceptance gates

1. **Prerequisite core correctness:** finish run-owned live books, single journal, explicit listing identity and complete ordinary-order replay before attempting market realism. A simulator that misaccounts its own fills cannot calibrate execution P&L.
2. **Scheduler and observation contract:** introduce a deterministic event heap, distinct receive/process/publish/deliver timestamps, per-listing observation snapshots and checkpointed RNG. Acceptance: simultaneous-event ordering is stable; an agent never accesses a future public state; restart reproduces identical action/output hashes; ADR 0024 semantics unchanged unless separately amended.
3. **Background-flow baseline:** implement null Poisson and state-dependent queue-reactive generator as scenario-selected policies with bounded memory and participant accounts. Acceptance: cancellations only of owned orders; bounded event count; no negative queues; exposure/fees balance; seeded replay hashes identical; no latent bypass of risk or command admission.
4. **Model fidelity:** compare *held-out* simulated vs observed spread distribution, L1/L2 depth, order-type and cancel/submit mix, size, interarrival time, queue imbalance, returns/volatility clustering, market impact/reversion and trade/quote response. Freeze calibration inputs/splits and statistical distance definitions before inspecting holdout. Prefer simplest passing model; report mismatches rather than asserting market realism.
5. **Strategy-faithful agents:** test model equations and quote/cancel trajectories against isolated numerical fixtures (including edge cases); institutional execution must account for fills, cash, costs, pending orders, and real available depth. Cross-venue arb must never profit from same-instant publication that was unavailable at its observation time.
6. **Scale and soak:** execute a proposed five-day/three-venue/cross-listing/50–100-agent experiment with mid-run crash/restart and full independent scoring; measure total durable events/sec, p50/p99 transition and commit latency, allocations, peak RSS, disk writes, event backlog, spread/depth stylized facts and economic/replay invariants. Compare Bunting on its original and improved implementation under equal inputs; no external advertised throughput becomes a release goal automatically.

### Prohibited shortcuts / evidence rules

- No second production matcher or direct orderbook mutation by agents; no hidden market maker privilege or fabricated fills.
- No copy of AGPL/unlicensed code without approved licensing/provenance; the research links do not constitute authorization to vendor source or add Git submodules. Recheck original license files before adoption.
- No causal access to future state, hidden market data or future shocks; no stochastic behavior tied to wall clock, thread schedule or unlogged random sources.
- No unbenchmarked throughput promises; note that the cited projects have different measurement scopes. Verify correctness before micro-optimizing custom allocators or concurrency.
- Do not treat this note as an accepted ADR or a change to the October 7 roadmap. Document and approve architecture changes separately where they conflict with accepted decisions.
